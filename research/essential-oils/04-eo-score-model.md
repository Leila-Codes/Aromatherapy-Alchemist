# Base Statistics
From all this information we can start to generate 'effect' categories. These will be split into two parts:
**Physiological** 
Physical or measurable effects on the body. These include anti-inflammatory actions, bronchodilation, antimicrobial effects, warming/cooling sensations, digestive support, and sedative effects.
**Emotional**
Psychological or experiential effects that may influence a user's mood, stress or emotional state. These include calming, relaxing, uplifting, anxiolytic, or stress-reducing effects. These outcomes are more subjective but widely reported in aromatherapy literature.

## Physiological Effect Categories
1. Sleep - Demonstratable sedative effects - assisting with improved sleep habits or quality.
    - Linalool (+5)
    - Cedrol (+4)
    - Patchoulol (+1)

2. Decongestant - Triggers bronchodilation and reduces inflammation in the airways to help a user breathe better - especially while suffering from a cold or flu.
    - 1,8-Cineole (+5)
    - Menthol / Menthone (+5)

3. Warming - If applied to skin it creates a temporary and localised warming effect.
    - Cinnamaldehyde (+5)

4. Cooling - If applied to skin it creates a temporary and localised cooling effect.
    - Menthol (+5)
    - Camphor (+5)

5. Antimicrobial - The oil can inhibit or kill certain micro-organisms such as bacteria, fungi or viruses. This can reduce risk of infection when applied on the skin or be used in surface cleaning applications.
    - Terpinen‑4‑ol (+5)
    - Cinnamaldehyde (+5)
    - Limonene (+2)
    - 1,8-Cineole (+3)
    - Geraniol (+3)
    - Citronellol (+2)
    - Geranial (+1)

6. Digestive relief - An oil that may help alleviate ailments related to the digestive system, by relaxing it or alleviating nausea symptoms.
    - Cinnamaldehyde (+5)
    - Menthol (+5)
    - Menthone (+3)
    - Chamomile (+2)
    - Geraniol (+2)
